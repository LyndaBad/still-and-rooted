A NAME WORTH KNOWING

Meet El Shaddai.

אֵל שַׁדַּי

God Almighty

Say it: el shad-EYE

El means God. Shaddai is traditionally rendered Almighty. The word’s precise origin is debated. “All-sufficient” and “more than enough” are familiar devotional explanations, rather than an undisputed literal etymology.

The full expression occurs seven times in the Hebrew Old Testament. Shaddai appears 48 times in total, including those seven. Genesis 49:25 uses Shaddai in the standard Hebrew text; it is included in the wider list.

The New Testament uses Greek terms for “Almighty”; those are related themes, not additional occurrences of the Hebrew name.

Read in context

Ask who is speaking, to whom, and what has happened. In Job, a character’s claim is not automatically God’s verdict. Read the speeches alongside God’s response in Job 38–42, including 42:7.

Promises to Abraham and his descendants have a specific covenant setting. Reflect on God’s character without turning every detail into a guarantee of personal wealth, children or an easy life.
